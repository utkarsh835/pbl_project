Stock Market Prediction

"Hypothesis/Project Statement: To develop a predictive framework that synergizes CNN-based spatial feature extraction from visual candlestick patterns with LSTM temporal modeling to improve the accuracy of short-term stock price forecasting."

Problem Statement

Market participants often rely on either technical indicators or price action (candlesticks), but rarely both in an automated, deep-learning context. This project addresses the difficulty of quantifying psychological market sentiment represented by candlestick formations while simultaneously accounting for long-term historical price trends.

Literature Review / Market Research

Recent research (e.g., Ramadhan et al., 2022) highlights that while standalone CNNs can identify candlestick patterns with over 90% accuracy, they lack the temporal context to predict long-term trends. Conversely, traditional LSTM models (as seen in Lu et al., 2020) excel at time-series forecasting but often miss the immediate market 'sentiment' signaled by visual price action. State-of-the-art hybrid models now focus on Feature Fusion—integrating Gramian Angular Field (GAF) encoded images with raw OHLC data to achieve a more holistic market analysis than either model could provide individually.

Recent research indicates a shift from basic models to hybrid architectures for stock forecasting. Lu et al. (2020) proved that LSTMs excel at capturing long-term trends, reaching correlation scores up to 97.2%, but often struggle with market "noise". Conversely, Ramadhan et al. (2022) showed that CNNs identify visual candlestick patterns with over 90.7% accuracy, yet they lack the temporal memory to predict future values. To bridge this, state-of-the-art studies by Qi et al. (2024) and Nguyen et al. (2025) utilize Hybrid CNN-LSTM models. In this framework, CNN layers act as "eyes" to extract features and filter noise, while LSTM layers act as "memory" to model the price trend. This fusion achieves high price proximity accuracy (94.5%+) and better generalization than individual vanilla models.

Research Gap / Innovation

Most models process raw price data as simple numerical sequences. Our innovation involves a multimodal approach: using CNNs to identify 'Doji', 'Hammer', or 'Engulfing' patterns within the data, and feeding those spatial insights into an LSTM to weigh their significance over time, creating a more 'trader-like' automated logic.

Traditional models primarily treat stock prices as simple numerical sequences, often failing to capture the visual "clues" that human traders rely on for decision-making. Our innovation addresses this by implementing a multimodal approach. We utilize CNN layers to act as "eyes" for spatial feature extraction, identifying high-impact visual patterns like 'Doji', 'Hammer', or 'Engulfing' candles within the price action. These spatial insights are then integrated into an LSTM network, which serves as a "memory" to weigh their significance over a 60-day temporal window. By synergizing these layers, our framework mimics trader-like automated logic, analyzing both immediate chart shapes and long-term trends to achieve a more holistic and accurate market forecast than standalone vanilla models.

System Methodology

Dataset / Input

Data is sourced via the Yahoo Finance (yfinance) API, focusing on high-volume equities (e.g., NIFTY 50 or AAPL) from 2015 to 2025. The dataset consists of ~2,500 daily records. Preprocessing involves Min-Max Scaling to a [0,1] range, handling missing values via linear interpolation, and a Sliding Window Transformation (60-day lookback). Critically, the input includes engineered features for Candlestick Pattern recognition (Open, High, Low, Close relationships).

Model / Architecture

The system utilizes a Hybrid CNN-LSTM Architecture. A 1D-Convolutional Layer acts as the primary feature extractor to identify spatial 'Candlestick' formations and local trends. The output is fed into a stacked LSTM (Long Short-Term Memory) network with 50 units each to learn long-term temporal dependencies. To prevent overfitting, Dropout layers (0.2) are integrated. The final dense layer uses a Linear activation function to regress the predicted price for the next trading session.
